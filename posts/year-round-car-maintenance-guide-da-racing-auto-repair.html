Why Year-Round Maintenance Matters

Many drivers think of maintenance as a once-a-year event — an oil change here, a tire rotation there. But vehicles degrade gradually, and problems develop quietly. A hose that looks fine in cool weather may fail in summer heat. Brakes that performed well in dry conditions may reveal weaknesses in the rain.

Year-round maintenance is about anticipation. By addressing wear and seasonal stressors proactively, you prevent breakdowns, preserve performance, and protect your investment. It's far cheaper to maintain a car than to repair it after a failure.

Spring: Recovery and Inspection

Spring is the season of renewal — and a great time to inspect your car after winter's demands.

What to Check

  • Brakes: Winter driving stresses brake components. Have pads, rotors, and fluid inspected.
  • Suspension: Potholes and rough roads take a toll. Check for worn shocks, struts, and bushings.
  • Tires: Inspect tread depth and sidewalls for damage. Consider rotating or replacing as needed.
  • Underbody: Road salt and debris can cause corrosion. A thorough wash and inspection helps.
  • Fluids: Top up or replace fluids that may have degraded over winter.

Summer: Heat and Long Drives

Summer brings heat, long road trips, and heavy AC use. Your car's cooling and electrical systems face extra strain.

What to Check

  • Cooling system: Radiator, hoses, coolant level, and thermostat should all be inspected. Overheating is a leading cause of summer breakdowns.
  • AC system: Ensure refrigerant levels are correct and the system is blowing cold.
  • Battery: Heat accelerates battery degradation. Have it tested before it fails.
  • Tires: Hot roads increase tire pressure and wear. Check pressure regularly and inspect for damage.
  • Oil: Use the right oil viscosity for summer temperatures and change it on schedule.

Fall: Preparation and Prevention

Fall is the ideal time to prepare your car for colder months ahead. Preventive maintenance now prevents winter headaches later.

What to Check

  • Battery: Cold weather is hard on batteries. Test and replace if necessary.
  • Tires: Consider winter tires if you face cold, wet, or snowy conditions. Check tread depth.
  • Wipers and lights: Shorter days and rain demand clear visibility. Replace worn wipers and check all lights.
  • Heating system: Make sure your heater and defroster work properly before you need them.
  • Brakes: Slippery conditions demand reliable stopping power. Inspect pads, rotors, and fluid.

Winter: Protection and Reliability

Winter is the most demanding season for vehicles. Cold starts, slippery roads, and reduced visibility all test your car's systems.

What to Check

  • Battery and charging system: Cold cranking power matters most in winter.
  • Tires: Winter tires or all-seasons in good condition are essential for grip.
  • Fluids: Ensure antifreeze is at the correct concentration and windshield washer fluid is rated for cold.
  • Brakes: Inspect frequently — winter driving stresses braking systems.
  • Lights and visibility: Keep headlights, taillights, and wipers in top condition.
